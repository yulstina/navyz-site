A Beginner’s Guide to Choosing the Right POS Handheld Devices

Evaluate speed, durability, payments, and connectivity before you buy for the whole floor.

February 18, 2025

What a handheld actually changes

A handheld moves the register to the table. The order goes to the kitchen while the server is still standing there, and payment happens without a second trip. On a busy floor that is one or two extra turns a night, which is the whole argument for buying one.

Battery and weight beat spec sheets

A device that dies at 8pm is worse than no device. Look for a full dinner service on one charge, a spare battery or a fast dock, and a body light enough to hold for six hours. Processor speed is rarely the constraint; the hand is.

Payments should be built in

Tap, chip, and mobile wallets on the device itself. If the reader is a separate dongle, you have added a thing to lose and a step to explain to every new hire.

Test the connection where it will fail

Walk the patio, the back corner, and the walk-in door with the device before you commit. Handhelds fail at the edges of the Wi-Fi, not in the middle of the dining room.

Buy one, then buy the rest

Run a single unit for two weeks with your most skeptical server. If they ask for a second one, you have your answer.
