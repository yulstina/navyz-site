Essential Features to Look for in a Restaurant Mobile POS System

Focus on the handful of features that actually improve accuracy, service, payments, and reporting.

February 4, 2025

It has to work when the internet does not

Offline mode is not a luxury feature. A mobile POS that can take and hold orders through a dropped connection, then sync when it returns, is the difference between a slow night and a closed one.

Modifiers that match how you actually cook

Most order errors start at the modifier screen. Nested options, required choices, and clear kitchen-facing names matter far more than how the interface looks in a demo.

One inventory, everywhere

When an item sells out it should disappear from the table, the kiosk, and the online ordering page at the same moment. Anything less creates a refund and an apology.

Reporting you will read

Sales by hour, by item, and by employee, available on your phone the next morning. If you have to export a spreadsheet to answer a basic question, you will stop asking it.

Permissions and shift handling

Voids, discounts, and drawer access should be tied to roles. This is the least exciting feature on the list and the one that pays for itself first.
